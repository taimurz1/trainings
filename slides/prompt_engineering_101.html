Slide 02 / 11 Core Concept

What is a Prompt?

A prompt is your set of instructions, context, and guardrails given to an AI. Better inputs lead directly to better outputs.

Key Takeaway: Stop treating AI like a search engine keyword bar. Treat it like a highly capable junior assistant.
Slide 03 / 11 Framework

The RTF Architecture

  • R (Role): Who should the AI pretend to be?
  • T (Task): What specific action must it complete?
  • F (Format): How should the output be structured?
Slide 04 / 11 Technique

Zero-Shot vs. Few-Shot Prompting

Provide 1 to 3 explicit examples in your prompt to teach the model your exact target layout or tone before asking for output.

Slide 05 / 11 Control

Negative Prompting & Constraints

Eliminate corporate buzzwords, force exact word limits, and specify what the AI must not do.

Slide 06 / 11 Reasoning

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting

Instruct the model to "think step-by-step" to drastically reduce hallucination in logical or mathematical tasks.

Slide 07 / 11 Audience

Persona & Tone Adaptation

Reframe complex technical ideas for executive audiences, students, or non-technical stakeholders instantly.

Slide 08 / 11 Structure

Delimiters & Input Tagging

Use tags like <context> or <document> to separate your instructions clean from raw source data.

Slide 09 / 11 Optimization

Self-Correction & Quality Audits

Ask the AI to critique its own draft against your requirements before giving you the final answer.

Slide 10 / 11 Summary

Key Action Steps

  1. Always define a clear Role, Task, and Format.
  2. Provide high-quality examples when layout matters.
  3. Iterate and refine instructions rather than starting over.
